# Lesson 16: Solution notes

The shared function is the whole game: two intake paths with two copies of the normalisation logic will diverge within a month (one trims, one forgets), and the keys diverge with them, and the duplicates return wearing a disguise of diligence. In n8n terms this is why the normaliser of Lesson 06 and the key computation belong in one place per door at most, and in a sub-workflow (Lesson 22's workflow tools) once several doors exist.

A collision note for the contract: truncating SHA-256 to 16 hex characters gives 64 bits, ample for a clinic's lead volume, and the contract should say what happens on a true collision (the second record is treated as the same person, which at this scale is the safe failure) rather than pretend collisions are impossible.

**What to notice.** Identity is a designed artefact. Systems do not "have" duplicates; they manufacture them, one unnormalised form at a time, and they stop the day someone owns the key.
